TECHNICAL REPORT & SYSTEM GUIDE

A closer look at an image’s visual and frequency signature.

GenAI Fingerprint (GenAIFP) is an open-source forensic research prototype exploring fine-grained engine attribution. While most detection tools perform binary classification (Real vs. AI), GenAIFP investigates whether fusing self-supervised vision foundation representations with spatial-frequency noise residuals can identify which specific generative model synthesized an image, or whether it originated from authentic camera photography.

01THE ANALYSIS

The 524-dimensional hybrid feature pipeline

Generative architectures imprint artifacts at two distinct levels: semantic visual tendencies (composition, lighting, color palette) and low-level physical frequency traces (upsampling checkerboards, Fourier spikes, deconvolution patterns). GenAIFP combines both into a single 524-dimensional feature vector.

  1. 01

    Prepare the image & luminance conversion

    The image is decoded to RGB, center-cropped to 512 × 512 pixels (smaller inputs are reflection-padded), and converted to grayscale luminance via standard ITU-R BT.601 coefficients:

    Luminance (ITU-R BT.601): Y = 0.299·R + 0.587·G + 0.114·B
  2. 02

    Extract visual foundation embeddings (384 dims)

    Pretrained DINOv2 ViT-S/14 (vit_small_patch14_dinov2.lvd142m) processes a resized view of the image crop through self-supervised transformer attention. The output is an L2-normalized 384-dimensional [CLS] vector capturing semantic geometry, lighting distribution, textural motifs, and color gradients.

  3. 03

    Extract spatial-frequency noise residuals (128 dims)

    The luminance channel is convolved with a 3 × 3 Steganographic Rich Model (SRM) linear high-pass filter to strip semantic edges and isolate microscopic sensor and generative residual noise:

    SRM High-Pass 3×3 Kernel: [ -1 2 -1 ] [ 2 -4 2 ] [ -1 2 -1 ]

    A 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) converts the residual to frequency space, shifts the zero-frequency DC component to the center, and computes the 2D log-power spectrum:

    2D Log-Power Spectrum: S(u, v) = log(1 + |F(u, v)|²)

    The upper-triangular symmetric spectrum is compressed into 128 dimensions via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) fitted on training data.

  4. 04

    Measure physical frequency scalars (12 metrics)

    Twelve hand-designed physical measurements capture frequency-domain structure across radial and azimuthal projections:

    • Peak-to-Average Spectral Ratio (PASR): Detects isolated periodic Fourier spikes caused by transposed convolution and attention lattice stitching.
    • Spectral Slope (α) & Intercept (β): Linear regression parameters fitted to the high-frequency tail of Radially Averaged Power Spectral Density (RAPSD).
    • Directional Anisotropy (Azimuthal Variance): Quantifies directional power imbalance across quadrants.
    • 8 Radial Octave Bands: Energy concentration partitioned from DC to Nyquist limits.
  5. 05

    Calibrated classification & out-of-distribution scoring

    The concatenated 524-number vector (384 visual + 128 spectral PCA + 12 frequency scalars) is classified by an XGBoost gradient-boosted decision tree. Raw logits are calibrated using Platt temperature scaling so output scores correspond to empirical likelihoods:

    Platt Temperature Probability Scaling (T = 0.935): P(y_i) = exp(z_i / T) / Σ_j exp(z_j / T)

    An Out-of-Distribution (OOD) distance detector gates predictions at threshold 0.677. Samples that deviate significantly from known target manifolds are flagged as out-of-distribution (is_ood: true).

03DATA & PROVENANCE

Dataset provenance & partitioning

Real-data training is executed in Kaggle using curated shards from OwensLab’s CommunityForensics and CommunityForensics-Small datasets (source shards: HFCF_Commercial_0, HFCF_Commercial_1, HFCF_small_174).

01 Shard Ingestion

Read raw image bytes and source metadata from Parquet shards; de-duplicate identical image bytes using cryptographic hashing.

02 Prompt Grouping

Group samples by normalized prompt hash to ensure images generated from identical prompts never cross train, calibration, or test splits.

03 Balanced Caps

Enforce strict caps of 100 clean examples per target class (1,200 total) to prevent large classes from dominating loss gradients.

04 OOD Calibration

Generate 1,200 paired degraded copies (JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, downscale/upscale) to tune temperature scaling and OOD gates.

Evaluation boundary & upstream provenance

In the source dataset, commercial-source rows are marked test while real-camera images are marked train. The splits used here are an internal prompt-grouped research evaluation, not the dataset's official benchmark score. The dataset does not expose generation-batch IDs or individual source-photo IDs, so latent batch-level correlations cannot be completely ruled out.

Why certain models separate cleanly

Authentic camera images lack synthetic upsampling periodicity. Firefly engines utilize distinct proprietary training sets and pipeline post-processing. DALL·E 2 leaves unmistakable high-frequency spectral roll-offs from unCLIP/GLIDE diffusion upsamplers. Midjourney V6.1 clusters strongly due to specialized aesthetic micro-textures.

Why modern diffusion models cross-confuse

Contemporary engines—such as DALL·E 3, Imagen 3, Ideogram, and FLUX—converge on similar architectural choices: SDXL/FLUX-style VAE decoders, flow matching / rectified schedules, and shared CLIP/T5 text embeddings. Their frequency residuals overlap, making fine-grained discrimination challenging.

05ARCHITECTURE

System implementation & deployment

GenAIFP is engineered for standalone local execution and lightweight containerization without heavy cloud infrastructure.

Local CLI & web interface

main.py serves as the primary CLI router for prediction, synthetic demos, and verification. The web app (src/genaifp/web.py) runs on FastAPI and Uvicorn with loopback isolation (127.0.0.1), 20 MB / 40 MP upload limits, and zero external client CDNs.

Containerization & Coolify

A production-ready Dockerfile and docker-compose.yml are optimized for CPU deployments using lightweight CPU PyTorch wheels (~200 MB rather than 2.5 GB CUDA wheels), reverse proxy support (GENAIFP_ALLOW_REMOTE=1), and automated /api/health checks.

06LIMITS & ETHICS

Operational boundaries & research disclaimers

Understanding what GenAIFP cannot do is as critical as understanding what it does.

In-memory privacy guarantees

The web service accepts requests locally. Image bytes are decoded, cropped, and analyzed entirely in RAM and immediately released. Uploaded files are never written to disk, cached, or transmitted to any remote cloud endpoint.

Perturbation sensitivity

Severe JPEG compression, social media messaging re-encoding (e.g., WhatsApp, Twitter/X), downscale-upscale resampling, or screenshots erase delicate high-frequency residuals. Under heavy degradation, models frequently misattribute classes without triggering OOD uncertainty.

Not legal or forensic evidence

Predictions are experimental research estimates. A high attribution probability indicates mathematical proximity in the 524-dimensional feature space to training shards, not verified authorship, authenticity, or legal proof of origin. Unsupported generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5, Midjourney V4, Grok) are caught by the OOD gate at modest recall (~15.5%) and may receive false high-confidence attributions. Never use GenAIFP as sole evidence in legal, journalistic, employment, or disciplinary proceedings.
